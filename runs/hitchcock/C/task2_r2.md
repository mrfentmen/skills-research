# Lighthouse Keeper Suspense Treatment

## Information Map

- **Audience knows:** The lamp filament is fractured, the light is dying, a ship is off course toward the rocks. The keeper has no idea the light has failed.
- **Character (keeper) knows:** The lamp is steady, the sea looks normal, he is focused on winding the clockwork mechanism. He believes the light is strong.

## Storyboard Beats

Beat 1: The flicker. The audience sees the lamp filament glow unevenly. The keeper is inside, polishing the lens, humming. The ship is far out on the horizon, its course true in the keeper's view but wrong from above.

Beat 2: The darkness. The lamp cuts out entirely. The audience watches the ship turn broadside to the rocks, the captain unaware. The keeper steps outside, puzzled, sees the unlit tower, and frowns.

Beat 3: The storm breaks. Wind howls, rain sheets the glass. The ship is perilously close to the rocks now. The keeper finally grasps the light is out, scrambles for the backup lamp, but the mechanism jams. The audience feels the inevitability.

## Ticking Clock Beats

1. **First tick:** The filament shows stress, a single uneven glow. The ship is distant, the night still.
2. **Second tick:** The light extinguishes. The ship has turned, the rocks are visible through the captain's portholes. The keeper is outside, bewildered.
3. **Third tick:** The storm arrives, the ship is upon the rocks, the keeper's frantic winding fails. The clock chimes the hour of consequence.

## Withheld Reveal

We never show the ship crashing against the rocks. The imagination supplies the shriek of wood on stone and the silence that follows. Hiding the moment is stronger because the audience's mind fills in a worse collision than any staged shot could.

## Earned Payoff

The clock goes off at the exact moment the keeper lifts the backup lamp the one he wound in beat one and the light cuts through the rain. The payoff lands because the flicker was planted in beat one and the darkness was sealed in beat two. The audience feels the relief of the earned restoration.

## McGuffin

- **The lens:** Everyone chases the light, thinking it is the thing that saves the ship.
- **What actually matters:** The keeper's decision to keep the lamp wound, the habit that outlasts the emergency.

## Scene Treatment

FADE IN:

EXT. LIGHTHOUSE, NIGHT

Rain spits against the glass. The lantern room glows a steady yellow eye against the storm.

The KEEPER (50s, weathered hands) winds the clockwork mechanism. The gears GRIND softly. He hums an old tune.

INSIDE the lantern room, the lamp flickers. The filament is thin, stressed. The audience sees it. The keeper does not.

OUTSIDE, the sea rolls dark under the wind. A SHIP appears on the horizon, its sails dark against the moon.

The keeper steps back from the lens. He squints at the light.

KEEPER
(softly)
Steady now. Steady.

The lamp EXTINGUISHES. Total darkness in the lantern room.

The keeper stares. He reaches for the backup. His hands slip on the oil.

A flash of lightning reveals the ship has turned. The rocks gleam white just ahead.

The keeper finally sees the darkness. He lunges for the valve.

The lamp reignites. Just as the ship's horn blasts a single mournful note against the rocks.

CUT TO BLACK.
