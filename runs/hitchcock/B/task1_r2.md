# Suspense Scene Treatment: "Wrong Bag"

## Storyboard

**SCENE 1 - INT. TRAIN CAR - DAY**

The train hums along tracks stretching into the horizon. MR. HARRIGAN (50s, weary but sharp-eyed) walks the aisles, checking tickets. The camera lingers on the window view—rolling fields, occasional farmhouse.

**SCENE 2 - INT. TRAIN CAR - CONTINUOUS**

ALEX (30s, nervous, clutching a coffee) sits by the window. Across the aisle, an elderly woman dozes with a heavy overnight bag beside her. Harrigan pauses at Alex's seat.

HARRIGAN
Ticket, please.

Alex hands over a ticket. Harrigan scans it, nods, moves on. But his eyes linger on the elderly woman's bag—something about the shape feels *off*.

**SCENE 3 - INT. TRAIN CAR - MOMENTS LATER**

Harrigan circles back. He stops beside Alex. 

HARRIGAN
Mind if I take a look?

ALEX
(Startled)
Look at what?

HARRIGAN
The bag next to you. The brown leather one.

ALEX
Oh. Yes, sure. Go ahead.

Harrigan lifts the bag's tag. Inside—NOT Alex's belongings. A chaos of unfamiliar items: a child's toy, a stack of passports with different names, a folded wad of foreign currency.

ALEX (CONT'D)
(Whispering)
That's not mine.

HARRIGAN
No. It isn't.

**SCENE 4 - INT. TRAIN CAR - LATER**

Harrigan radios the conductor's car. The train is approaching the next station. We see the elderly woman stirring, disoriented, searching for her bag—it's gone. Panic sets in.

**SCENE 5 - INT. TRAIN CAR - CLIMAX**

Harrigan confronts Alex. Not with accusation—with protection. He's noticed the mix-up at the last stop. Someone swapped bags intentionally. The real owner is still on board, unaware.

HARRIGAN
We need to offload this at the next station. Quietly.

Alex nods. The camera PUSH IN on Harrigan's eyes—calm, alert, determined.

**SCENE 6 - INT. TRAIN CAR - RESOLUTION**

At the next stop, Harrigan and Alex discreetly switch bags off the train. The elderly woman boards, finds her bag, relief washing over her. Harrigan tips his conductor's cap—a small gesture, but in this moment, it means everything.

The train pulls away. Harrigan returns to his walk. The camera EASES OUT as the rhythm of the tracks resumes.

## Notes

- **Tone**: Hitchcockian suspense—ordinary setting, extraordinary danger, the tension of *knowing something the characters don't*
- **Visual motifs**: The bag as a shifting mystery object, the train window as a frame for normality masking threat, Harrigan's conductor's cap as both uniform and shield
- **Key insight**: The suspense comes not from the bag itself, but from the conductor's awareness—and the quiet responsibility that comes with it
