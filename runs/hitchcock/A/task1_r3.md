# Suspense Scene Treatment: Bag Switch

## Title: MIDNIGHT SWAP

### Logline
A weary train conductor discovers a passenger's luggage has been swapped — but the implications of the switch threaten more than just a ruined vacation.

### Setting
- **Train:** Night Owl Express, Route 7, traveling from Boston to Miami
- **Time:** 2:14 AM, December
- **Carriage:** C — the quiet car

### Characters
- **Elliott (50s)** — seasoned conductor, tired but sharp-eyed, thinks he's seen it all
- **MARTHA (30s)** — passenger in Seat 12C, anxious, clutching a small overnight bag
- **DIRECTOR (unseen)** — the voice on the radio, calm and unsettling

### Beat Board (Storyboard)

**BEAT 1 — THE ROUTINE**
[Panel 1] Interior train car — dim emergency lighting. Elliott walks the aisle, clipboard in hand. The train hums.
[Panel 2] Close on Elliott's boots stepping on the metal floor. He glances at his watch: 2:10.
[Panel 3] Martha in 12C, staring out the window at passing headlights. She hugs her bag.

**BEAT 2 — THE DISCOVERY**
[Panel 4] Elliott stops at 12C. Martha looks up, startled.
[Panel 5] Elliott: "Ticket, please."
[Panel 6] Martha hands over her pass. Elliott scans it. His expression shifts — a flicker of something.

**BEAT 3 — THE REALIZATION**
[Panel 7] Close-up: Martha's bag. A luggage tag slips free — reveals a different destination sticker underneath: "PORTLAND" not "MIAMI."
[Panel 8] Elliott's POV — he checks the overhead bin. Seat 12A (empty) has a bag that *looks* like Martha's, but the tag reads "MIAMI."
[Panel 9] Tight on Elliott's eyes — recognition dawns. He steps back.

**BEAT 4 — THE CONFRONTATION**
[Panel 10] Elliott addresses Martha quietly. "You're not where your ticket says you are."
[Panel 11] Martha's face falls. She opens her bag — clothes, toiletries, a photo of a family she's visiting in Miami.
[Panel 12] Elliott: "Someone swapped bags at the last stop. You're sitting on someone else's belongings — and they're sitting on yours."

**BEAT 5 — THE IMPLICATION**
[Panel 13] Elliott pulls out the "other" bag from 12A. It's heavier than it looks. A slight bulge beneath the jacket.
[Panel 14] Cut to the radio: Director's voice. "Conductor, we have a situation. Keep her calm. Do not let her off at the next stop."
[Panel 15] Elliott looks at Martha. He knows more than he's letting on.

### Treatment
Elliott realizes the bag swap is no accident. The passenger in 12A — a man who boarded at the last minute, coat collar turned up, avoiding eye contact — has been carrying something that doesn't belong on a routine midnight run. Martha is an unwitting courier, or perhaps a target. Elliott has to decide: land the train at the next station and risk a security incident, or divert? The tension hinges on a man whose routine is about to be broken — and the quiet woman who just wants to get home to her daughter.

### Visual Mood
- Cold fluorescent strips against dark carriage
- Shadows lengthening as the train moves through winter countryside
- One suitcase sitting wrong, tag askew
- Elliott's badge reflecting the dim light
